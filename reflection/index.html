Coming into the course

When I first came into this course, I mostly thought about genre as the type of writing I was doing, like a research paper or a persuasive essay. I knew that different assignments had different expectations, but I had never really thought about why those expectations existed. Throughout the three main projects, I started to see how much the audience and purpose affect the choices I make. This became clearer because I worked with the same topic, overtreatment in cosmetic dentistry, and had to figure out how to communicate it in several different ways.

The annotated bibliography

The annotated bibliography was where I first noticed a change in how I approached research. At the beginning, I was mainly looking for sources that had something to do with cosmetic dentistry. Once I started writing the annotations, I had to think about what each source actually helped me understand. For example, a source about social media and beauty standards helped me consider why someone might want a smile makeover in the first place. That gave me a different way to approach my research question. I could look at what happens before a patient even walks into a dental office and consider how those expectations might shape the conversation about treatment.

The Investigative Field Essay

While working on my Investigative Field Essay, I realized that I sometimes moved on from a piece of evidence before explaining why it mattered. The feedback I received pointed out places where I could include more direct evidence or introduce a source more clearly. When I went back through the essay, I started paying more attention to where a reader might have questions that I had already answered in my head. Sometimes I knew exactly how a source connected to my point, but I had left that connection out of the paragraph.

“Sometimes I knew exactly how a source connected to my point, but I had left that connection out of the paragraph.”
Poster, memes, and commentary

Creating a poster, memes, and a commentary made me think about how those same ideas would work in different genres. For my poster, When Does a Smile Makeover Become Overtreatment?, I had to decide what someone should take away if they only had time to glance at it. In the commentary, I had more room to explain how seeing similar smiles on social media can influence what people expect their own teeth to look like. Working on both made me more aware of how often I rely on adding another sentence to explain myself. With the poster, I had to use the title, layout, and placement of information to help the reader follow my point.
